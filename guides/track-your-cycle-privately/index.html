How to track your menstrual cycle privately

Last updated October 10, 2026

Pick a tracker that needs no account, makes no network calls and keeps your data on your phone. Check four things before you log anything.

Why cycle data is sensitive

A cycle log records when you bleed, how you feel and, in many apps, notes about your health and sex life. Taken together over months, it shows more about your body than most people share with anyone.

Where that record lives matters. Data stored only on your phone is limited to the people who can unlock the phone. Data copied to a company's servers is subject to that company's policies, security and legal exposure for as long as it stays there.

Four checks for any tracker

  1. Does it require an account? An account usually means your entries are tied to an email address and stored on a server. Look for an app that works from the first launch without signing up.
  2. Does it include third-party SDKs or analytics? SDKs are code from other companies bundled into the app. Check the app's privacy policy, and its source code if it is open.
  3. Does it make network calls? An app that never needs the internet cannot send your data anywhere. Look for a "Data Not Collected" label on the App Store page, and on iPhone review Settings › Privacy & Security › App Privacy Report after using the app for a few days.
  4. Do you control export and deletion? You should be able to delete entries instantly and share data only when you choose to.

A label or a policy is a claim. Open-source code lets anyone verify the claim.

Back up and switch phones

Because RhythmX stores data on the device, it has no cloud copy to restore from. Whether your entries move to a new phone depends on your iPhone backup, so confirm that your iCloud or computer backup includes app data before you switch or reset a device.

Limits

RhythmX is a tracking tool, not a medical device. Predictions are estimates based on the cycles you log, and they are not a form of contraception. For health concerns, talk to a healthcare professional. For what each phase typically involves, see the cycle phases reference.
